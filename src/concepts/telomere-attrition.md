# Telomere Attrition

*Source: [§3 of the paper](../sources/ijms-27-18-8080-fulltext.md)*

## Mechanisms

- Telomeres protect chromosome ends via **TTAGGG repeats, shelterin complex, T-loop architecture**, and telomeric chromatin.
- In somatic cells, incomplete end-replication + limited telomerase activity → telomeres shorten with each division; oxidative lesions and replication stress accelerate attrition.
- Critically short/uncapped telomeres activate persistent **ATM/ATR DNA-damage signaling** → p53–p21-mediated arrest, senescence, apoptosis, altered tissue repair.
- Telomere dysfunction connects genome maintenance, stem-cell reserve, senescence, and inflammation; most consequential in proliferative compartments.

## Evidence

- Human **telomere-biology disorders** (telomeres in pulmonary fibrosis, bone-marrow failure, liver disease, immunodeficiency) prove short/dysfunctional telomeres can be pathogenic.
- Mice: telomerase deficiency → progressive tissue degeneration; telomerase reactivation or gene transfer restores function in selected settings.
- **Caution:** lab mice have much longer telomeres than humans; telomerase-deficient models represent severe failure, not normal age dynamics.
- Leukocyte telomere length correlates with age-associated outcomes at the population level, but measurement variability, leukocyte composition effects, and reverse causation limit its use as a universal biomarker.

## Therapeutic landscape

- Approaches: treating defined telomere syndromes, reducing oxidative/inflammatory stress, **transient telomerase activation**.
- **Danazol** (prospective telomere-disorder study): hematologic response + reduced attrition, but androgenic/pleiotropic actions confound interpretation.
- **AAV-TERT** (systemic AAV delivery of telomerase reverse transcriptase) tested in adult/old mice and pulmonary-fibrosis models.
- **Central liability: cancer** — short telomeres normally constrain proliferation and suppress early tumor growth; reactivation may restore regeneration but can feed malignant clones.
- Translation priorities: genetically/clinically defined telomere failure, time-limited or tissue-directed delivery, combined endpoints.
