# Mitochondrial Dysfunction

*Source: [§8 of the paper](../sources/ijms-27-18-8080-fulltext.md)*

## Mechanisms

- Mitochondria integrate ATP production, redox control, biosynthesis, calcium handling, apoptosis, innate-immune signaling, and metabolite-dependent chromatin regulation.
- Aging alters respiratory-chain function, membrane potential, mitochondrial dynamics, mtDNA integrity, and organelle turnover — **direction and magnitude vary by tissue and physiological state**.
- **ROS are signaling molecules**, not just damage: low/transient mitochondrial stress induces adaptive **mitohormetic** responses; sustained/excessive stress causes damage. This dose-dependence explains why blanket antioxidant supplementation hasn't reproduced free-radical-theory predictions.
- Mitochondrial quality depends on coordinated **fission, fusion, biogenesis, proteostasis, and mitophagy** (**PINK1–Parkin**-dependent + receptor-mediated).
- Damaged mitochondria release **mtDNA, cardiolipin, formyl peptides** → activate **cGAS-STING, TLRs, inflammasomes**; inflammatory output depends on release route, amount, receiving cell, and countervailing mitophagy.
- Somatic **mtDNA mutations expand clonally** in some human tissues, but causal importance varies; total mutation burden doesn't directly predict functional decline.

## Interventions

- **Exercise**: best-established intervention for mitochondrial capacity + whole-body function.
- Candidates: **urolithin A** (mitophagy programs), **NAD+ precursors** (biochemical engagement, variable functional benefit), modulators of dynamics/quality control.
- Human studies show target engagement + selected metabolic/functional effects, but **no established slowing of organismal aging**.
- Evaluation should use tissue-appropriate bioenergetics, mtDNA/quality-control markers, and functional outcomes, accounting for **sex, training status, and baseline metabolic disease**.
