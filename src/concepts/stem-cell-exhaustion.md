# Stem Cell Exhaustion

*Source: [§10 of the paper](../sources/sensors-8080-fulltext.md)*

## Mechanisms

- Tissue maintenance depends on resident stem/progenitor cells, **facultative plasticity**, supportive niches, and systemic signals.
- Aging alters stem-cell **abundance, quiescence, self-renewal, lineage output, and injury responses** — patterns differ sharply by tissue:
  - **Hematopoietic stem cells**: expand numerically but show **myeloid bias, clonal selection**, and impaired regenerative function.
  - **Muscle satellite cells / neural progenitors**: lose activation or differentiation competence.
  - **Epithelial tissues**: retain turnover while accumulating **mutant clones**.
- Contributing factors: telomere dysfunction, epigenetic drift, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired proteostasis, inflammation, and ECM changes act through cell-autonomous + niche-dependent mechanisms.

## Key framing

- Stem-cell exhaustion is an **integrative outcome**, not a primary lesion.
- Restoring **autophagy, metabolic regulation, or niche signaling** improves stem-cell function/regeneration in mice.
- Distinct risks: expansion can favor **oncogenic clones**; transplantation may replace a deficient compartment **without correcting the aged niche** or systemic environment.
- Clinical translation must define the goal (cell number vs lineage output vs injury response vs durable organ function), and monitor **clonal diversity, mutation burden, and malignancy** whenever regenerative capacity is increased.
