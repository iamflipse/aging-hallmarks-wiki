# Dysbiosis & Barrier Dysfunction

*Source: [§13 of the paper](../sources/sensors-8080-fulltext.md)*

## Gut microbiome in aging

- The gut microbiome drives nutrient processing, **SCFA production**, bile-acid transformation, epithelial integrity, immune education, and xenobiotic metabolism.
- Later life brings changes in **community structure, ecological stability, and microbial metabolites** — but trajectories vary markedly among individuals/populations (diet, medications, disease, environment).
- **No universal "aged microbiome" exists**; **functional output** is more informative than taxonomic diversity/composition.
- **Animal-transfer evidence**: microbiota from young donors improves immune, reproductive, behavioral, or survival outcomes in aged/progeroid recipients.
- Human evidence is less conclusive: disease/meds/physiology can induce dysbiosis; microbial shifts may reflect **compensation rather than causation**.
- **Barrier failure** (mucus/tight-junction loss) → increased exposure to **LPS, bacterial DNA** → pattern-recognition receptors + systemic inflammation.
- Microbiome effects extend beyond the gut: **SCFAs, secondary bile acids, tryptophan metabolites, TMA-related pathways, vagal signaling, immune modulation**.

## Interventions

- **Dietary fiber, prebiotics, probiotics, postbiotics, FMT** can modify microbial function/structure.
- Efficacy established for a limited set of indications — **not for aging itself**.
- Responses depend on baseline ecology, diet, medication, host genotype, donor characteristics.
- Safety concerns: pathogen transmission, antimicrobial-resistance genes, unpredictable metabolic effects.
- Geroscience trials must define **which microbial function** is being modified, verify the metabolite/barrier pathway, and connect it to host physiology.

## Figure 3 (paper)

- (A) Intestinal homeostasis: fiber → SCFAs → epithelial integrity + immune function.
- (B) Age-related microbial shifts alter metabolite production, weaken mucus/tight junctions, increase translocation; microbial products prime **NF-κB** transcription; host danger signals promote **NLRP3 → caspase-1 → IL-1β/IL-18 maturation**.
- (C) Resulting inflammation impairs metabolic, neural, muscular, and vascular function and further weakens the barrier — a vicious cycle modifiable by diet, activity, genotype, and environment.
