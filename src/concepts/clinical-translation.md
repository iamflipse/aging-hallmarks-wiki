# Clinical Translation

*Source: [§16 of the paper](../sources/ijms-27-18-8080-fulltext.md)*

## Levels of translational claim

Translation starts with a **clearly defined claim**. The hierarchy of claims:

1. **Target engagement** — does the intervention change the intended molecular process?
2. **Biomarker response** — does a prespecified mechanism-linked indicator change in the expected direction?
3. **Disease modification** — does it alter the course of a defined disease?
4. **Functional preservation** — mobility, cognition, sensory function, independence.
5. **Resilience** — the ability to respond to a challenge and recover from it.
6. **Modification of organismal aging** — the broadest claim: sustained, multi-domain benefit with acceptable safety.

**Key rule:** these levels are **not automatically sequential or substitutable** — evidence at a lower level does not establish a higher level; each transition needs its own design and endpoints. A single molecular surrogate cannot establish modification of organismal aging.

## Challenge–response–recovery framework

- Static state measures describe **resting** status; **dynamic resilience measures** quantify the response trajectory after a standardized perturbation: peak displacement, cross-system coordination, response duration, time to recovery, residual deficit, repeatability.
- Exercise, vaccination, surgery, and metabolic challenges reveal differences between individuals with similar resting biomarkers — connecting transient adaptation vs persistent dysfunction to measurable **physiological reserve**.

## Biomarker selection by context of use

- **Diagnostic** (identifies state), **prognostic** (predicts outcome), **predictive** (identifies likely response), **pharmacodynamic** (shows biological activity); **surrogate endpoints** only after stringent validation.
- Most proposed aging biomarkers are **prognostic or pharmacodynamic**, not validated surrogates.
- Function-focused measures: **gait speed, handgrip strength, cardiorespiratory fitness, cognition, frailty indices, post-challenge recovery** (vaccination, exercise, surgery, metabolic challenge).

## Stratification & treatment strategy

- Match the biomarker to the mechanism: a senescence therapy needs evidence of **senescent-cell burden**; a telomere intervention focuses on **telomere dysfunction**, not chronological age; mitochondrial interventions are most informative in participants with **impaired oxidative capacity**.
- Stratify by **sex, frailty, multimorbidity, medication, and baseline reserve**.
- **Intermittent dosing** may suit pathways where chronic inhibition impairs repair/immunity/metabolism.
- Follow-up must be long enough to catch delayed harms (cancer, infection, tissue-function loss).

## Staged translational program

1. **Early studies**: establish dose, safety, target engagement, and a mechanism-linked response in the relevant tissue (or a justified proxy).
2. **Phase 2 (mechanism-enriched RCTs)**: prespecified disease, functional, or challenge-recovery endpoint.
3. **Later-phase trials**: only interventions with concordant benefit + acceptable safety proceed to multidomain function, **multimorbidity incidence, disability-free survival**, or other validated composite outcomes.

## Precision geroscience

- Not indiscriminate personalization with big biomarker panels — instead: match the intervention to a **measurable, modifiable causal mechanism** in a defined individual/group, then test whether the predicted downstream benefit occurs.
- If target engagement does not improve function, the target may be **downstream, redundant, engaged at the wrong time, or group-specific**.
