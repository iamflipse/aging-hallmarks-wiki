Loss of Proteostasis

Source: §5 of the paper

Mechanisms

  • Proteostasis integrates protein synthesis, folding, trafficking, compartmentalization, repair, and degradation.
  • Players: molecular chaperones, ubiquitin–proteasome system, ER/mitochondrial unfolded-protein responses (UPR), chaperone-mediated autophagy, stress-granule dynamics.
  • Aging reduces capacity/coordination → oxidized, glycated, misfolded, aggregation-prone proteins accumulate.
  • Translation fidelity and elongation kinetics change with age → ribosome function directly affects proteome quality.
  • Proteostasis cannot be inferred from aggregate abundance alone — the unfolded-protein response can be adaptive when transient; prolonged eIF2α-ATF4 signaling becomes maladaptive (translation suppression, metabolic remodeling).
  • Aggregates may be toxic species, protective deposits, or markers of upstream failure.

Evidence

  • Genetic enhancement of chaperone activity, proteasome function, translation fidelity, chaperone-mediated autophagy, or selective macroautophagy extends lifespan in model organisms.
  • Chemical chaperones and UPR modulators tested in aged mice and proteotoxic disorders: indication-specific, mixed efficacy.

Pleiotropy constraints

  • Chronic suppression of protein synthesis impairs growth/repair; sustained stress signaling becomes maladaptive; enhanced proteostasis can help malignant cells survive.
  • Intervention design: identify the defective proteostasis step, demonstrate restored flux/fidelity, and monitor tissue repair, immunity, and cancer.
