# Deregulated Nutrient Sensing

*Source: [§7 of the paper](../sources/sensors-8080-fulltext.md)*

## Mechanisms

- Nutrient-sensing networks couple substrate availability to growth, reproduction, stress resistance, and maintenance.
- **Insulin/IGF-1 → PI3K–AKT** inhibits FOXO and drives anabolism; **amino acids → mTORC1**; **AMPK** responds to energy deficit and suppresses mTORC1; **sirtuins** link **NAD+** availability to deacetylation of metabolic/chromatin/stress-response proteins.
- Longevity effects of reduced insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS) or mTOR signaling **vary by pathway branch, tissue, sex, dose, developmental stage, and nutritional state**.

## Interventions

- **Caloric restriction** (without malnutrition): extends lifespan in several species; remodels IIS, mTOR, AMPK, autophagy, circadian, and inflammatory pathways. In humans: cardiometabolic/immune changes, but **no lifespan evidence yet**. Meal timing, protein/amino-acid composition, adiposity, and circadian alignment modify the response. Restriction can harm frail, sarcopenic, or undernourished individuals.
- **Rapamycin / mTOR inhibitors**: extend mouse lifespan; transient/low-dose regimens preserve function. **Phase 2b**: low-dose mTORC1 inhibition ↑ interferon-antiviral gene expression and reduced lab-confirmed respiratory infections; **phase 3 did not reduce clinically symptomatic respiratory illness** — a cautionary tale about endpoint definition and population selection.
- **Metformin**: evaluated as a geroscience intervention; alters energy metabolism and disease risk; **no evidence yet that it slows human aging**.
- **Acarbose**: extends mouse lifespan with **marked sex dependence**.
- **NAD+ precursors**: consistent biochemical changes, variable clinical outcomes.

## Principle

These interventions test **distinct components** of nutrient sensing — they are **not interchangeable geroprotectors**.
