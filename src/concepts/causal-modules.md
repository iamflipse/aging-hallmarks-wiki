# From Descriptive Hallmarks to Causal Modules

*Source: [§15 of the paper](../sources/sensors-8080-fulltext.md)*

## The three original hallmark criteria (López-Otín 2023)

1. Age-associated manifestation
2. Experimental **aggravation** accelerates aging
3. Experimental **amelioration** delays/attenuates aging

Sound, but insufficient unless "aging" and the outcome are explicitly defined — a change may be **causal, compensatory, or merely consequential**.

## Strong causal inference requires

- **Temporal ordering**, **dose–response**, **reversibility** (where biologically plausible), **mediation**, **replication across models**, and **convergence** of genetic, pharmacological, and environmental evidence.
- Human relevance: the mechanism must operate within **clinically accessible ranges**.
- A favorable inflammatory marker or epigenetic-clock response **does not** establish delayed disability/mortality.
- Disease-specific benefits can reduce multimorbidity but create liabilities elsewhere.
- **Mechanistic and clinical outcomes must be aligned prospectively**; claims stay at the level directly tested.

## "Provisional causal module" (operational definition)

> A bounded, experimentally tractable set of interacting processes for which **perturbation changes one or more prespecified age-relevant functional outcomes through an identifiable mechanism**.

- Requires more than association or prediction.
- A biomarker may signal without mediating a process; a downstream manifestation can change without affecting upstream determinants; compensatory responses can be beneficial or harmful depending on context/time.
- **Causal independence is neither expected nor required** — modules are partially overlapping, connected by **feedback, compensation, and resource allocation**.
- Examples of reciprocal feedback: DNA damage → senescence + inflammatory signaling; inflammation → impaired stem-cell function, mitochondrial quality, insulin sensitivity, proteostasis; mitochondrial dysfunction → innate immunity via mtDNA release; autophagy can restrain or amplify that response depending on lysosomal competence.
- The **primary / antagonistic / integrative** split is a **heuristic and temporal tendency**, not a fixed causal hierarchy — feedback runs in both directions.
- Transition from adaptation to dysfunction depends on challenge magnitude/duration/recurrence, baseline physiological reserve, regenerative capacity, damage-clearance efficiency, stress-response termination, and recovery completeness — explaining why the same process (mitochondrial stress, senescence, autophagy, inflammation) is **protective when transient, harmful when persistent**.
- Combination therapy: may be required in some contexts, but indiscriminate multi-targeting increases toxicity and obscures causal interpretation. Rational combinations need **demonstrated mechanistic interaction, nonredundant benefit, compatible PK/PD**, and prespecified safety criteria.
