# Epigenetic Alterations

*Source: [§4 of the paper](../sources/ijms-27-18-8080-fulltext.md)*

## Mechanisms

- Aging alters DNA methylation, histone modifications, nucleosome positioning, chromatin accessibility, 3D genome organization, transposable-element control, and noncoding-RNA programs.
- Changes are partly **stochastic** and partly **coordinated responses** to DNA damage, inflammation, metabolism, and cell-turnover shifts.
- Loss of **H3K9me3-rich heterochromatin** + derepression of repetitive elements → disrupted transcriptional homeostasis + activated innate-immune signaling (cGAS-STING, interferons).
- **Metabolite coupling:** DNMT/TET, histone acetyltransferases/deacetylases, and chromatin remodelers depend on **acetyl-CoA, SAM, NAD+, α-ketoglutarate** — connecting epigenetics to nutrient sensing, mitochondrial function, and DNA repair.
- Epigenetic changes are both **readouts and mediators** of aging-related dysfunction.

## Epigenetic clocks

- DNA-methylation clocks predict chronological age, mortality risk, or physiological state.
- 1st-generation clocks trained on chronological age; later models added mortality proteins, smoking, clinical phenotypes, causal/longitudinal/cross-species information.
- **Different clocks encode different information** — chronological-age, mortality, and mitotic clocks are not interchangeable.
- Cell composition, ancestry, cohort structure, and acute stress (pregnancy, surgery, infection, exercise) shift clock outputs; biomarker reversibility ≠ organismal-aging reversal.

## Partial reprogramming (OSK experiment)

- **OCT4-SOX2-KLF4 (OSK) ± MYC** can reset selected molecular features and improve vision/regeneration in mice.
- Cited study: **two-vector AAV9, doxycycline-inducible OSK**, systemic delivery to **124-week-old male C57BL/6J mice** → increased median remaining lifespan + improved frailty index (single preclinical experiment, males only).
- Risks: loss of cell identity, dysplasia, teratoma, oncogenesis.
- No human trial has established safety/efficacy of systemic partial reprogramming; human epigenetic-age studies remain exploratory.
